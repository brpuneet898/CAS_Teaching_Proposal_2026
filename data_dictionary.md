# ClimateTwin V2 Synthetic Dataset — Data Dictionary

## Purpose

`dataset.csv` is the student-facing synthetic portfolio for **ClimateTwin: An Actuarial Digital Twin for Air–Water Climate Risk Management**. It contains exactly **750 policy-location-year observations**: 250 synthetic commercial locations observed for policy years 2024–2026.

The V2 data system is generated by `data_script.py`, which is the **single source of truth** for policy terms, environmental conditions, insured triggers, claim frequency, claim-level severity, coverage calculations, scenario definitions, and validation checks.

The generator also creates an instructor-side supplementary table, `claim_level_data.csv`. That claim-level table is the authoritative source for severity modelling and for checking the relationship between ground-up loss, covered loss, deductible, policy limit, and insurer-paid loss. `dataset.csv` contains only annual aggregates of those claim-level records.

A central coverage principle is:

> **Environmental deterioration alone is not an insurance claim.**

An adverse air or water state must first satisfy the case's environmental screening rule and then produce a qualifying insured trigger. Even after a trigger exists, a location-year may have zero claims. A claim may also be covered but produce **zero insurer payment** because the covered amount is at or below the deductible. Those zero-paid claims are valid observations and are retained in V2.

---

## 1. Dataset grains and keys

### `dataset.csv`

- **Rows:** 750
- **Unique locations:** 250
- **Policy years:** 2024, 2025, 2026
- **Grain:** one insured location × one policy year
- **Unique row key:** `policy_id`
- **Repeated-location key:** `location_id`

### `claim_level_data.csv` generated by `data_script.py`

- **Grain:** one simulated covered claim
- **Unique key:** `claim_id`
- **Foreign key:** `policy_id`
- One policy-location-year may have zero, one, or multiple claim rows.
- Annual claim counts and annual loss totals in `dataset.csv` are calculated directly from this table.

## 4. Exact loss definitions and calculation order

V2 uses the following sequence for every individual claim.

### Step 1 — Ground-up remediation / extra expense

A positive claim-level remediation / extra-expense amount is generated.

### Step 2 — Ground-up BI

A claim-level interruption duration and positive daily BI amount are generated.

```text
ground_up_bi_loss = daily_bi_loss × interruption_days
```

### Step 3 — BI waiting period

The waiting period applies **only to the BI component**.

```text
covered_bi_days = max(interruption_days - bi_waiting_period_days, 0)
covered_bi_loss = daily_bi_loss × covered_bi_days
```

Therefore a claim can fail the BI waiting period and still contain covered remediation / extra expense.

### Step 4 — Ground-up loss

```text
ground_up_loss = ground_up_remediation_loss + ground_up_bi_loss
```

### Step 5 — Covered loss before financial terms

```text
covered_loss = ground_up_remediation_loss + covered_bi_loss
```

Thus, in V2:

```text
ground_up_loss >= covered_loss > 0
```

for every simulated claim.

### Step 6 — Deductible, exactly once

```text
loss_after_deductible = max(covered_loss - deductible, 0)
```

The deductible is not separately applied to remediation and BI.

### Step 7 — Per-claim policy limit

```text
paid_loss = min(loss_after_deductible, policy_limit)
```

Therefore:

```text
0 <= paid_loss <= policy_limit
```

A covered claim may have `paid_loss = 0` when the covered loss does not exceed the deductible. These are legitimate zero-paid claims and are intentionally retained.

Because the policy limit is per claim, an annual `aggregate_paid_loss` can exceed one policy limit if the policy-year contains multiple claims.

## 5. Severity-modelling rule for V2

The core severity model must **not** fit a Gamma distribution directly to `aggregate_paid_loss / claim_count`, because insurer-paid severity can legitimately be zero after the deductible.

For a positive continuous severity model such as Gamma or Lognormal, use the instructor-side claim-level response:

```text
covered_loss
```

or, if the teaching objective is pre-coverage economic loss:

```text
ground_up_loss
```

Both are strictly positive by construction.

`paid_loss` is a payment variable rather than a strictly positive severity variable. If an advanced class models paid severity directly, it should use a two-part/hurdle treatment or explicitly condition on `paid_loss > 0` and separately model payment probability.

## 6. Frequency mechanism in V2

V1 used a more brittle frequency specification with direct large effects attached to trigger indicators. V2 replaces this with a bounded exposure-adjusted mechanism.

The process is:

1. generate the environmental state;
2. calculate bounded continuous air and water hazard scores;
3. determine whether each environmental screening threshold is crossed;
4. draw air and water insured-trigger eligibility probabilities from those scores, occupancy, and mitigation;
5. if no insured trigger is active, claim frequency is zero;
6. if at least one trigger is active, generate an exposure-adjusted Poisson count using bounded continuous hazard scores, occupancy, climate, mitigation, and—only in the compound stress scenario—a non-zero compound interaction.

The implementation clamps the log-frequency predictor to a finite range before exponentiation. This prevents pathological rates and provides stable finite simulation diagnostics.

### Important modelling note

The trigger flags are primarily **coverage-audit variables**. Because claims are not permitted when no insured trigger exists, instructors should avoid using the trigger flags as the only predictors in the core frequency GLM. The preferred student model uses environmental, occupancy, climate, region, and mitigation predictors with `log(exposure_years)` as the offset.

---

## 7. Core environmental screening thresholds

The thresholds are transparent and unchanged across scenarios:

- **Air stress:** `pm25 >= 35` **or** `ozone >= 70`
- **Water stress:** `turbidity >= 5` **or** `water_quality_index <= 60`

Crossing a threshold creates an adverse environmental state only. It does **not** automatically create an insured trigger or claim.

## 8. Exact four-scenario definitions

All four scenarios operate on the same underlying 750 policy-location-year portfolio. Policy terms, occupancy, region, insured value, and existing mitigation are held fixed. The scenario engine uses **common random numbers**, so scenario comparisons reflect changed environmental conditions and model effects rather than unrelated simulation noise.

| Scenario | Air transformation | Water transformation | Compound interaction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Baseline** | Observed synthetic PM2.5 and ozone | Observed synthetic turbidity and WQI | No additional scenario interaction |
| **Air stress** | `PM2.5 + 15 µg/m³`; `ozone + 10 ppb` | Unchanged | No additional scenario interaction |
| **Water stress** | Unchanged | `turbidity × 1.60`; `WQI - 15 points` | No additional scenario interaction |
| **Compound stress** | Apply the air-stress transformation | Apply the water-stress transformation | If both insured triggers are active, add `+0.28` to log-frequency and multiply Compound-claim severity by `1.18` |

After each scenario transformation, air/water screening flags and insured-trigger probabilities are recalculated. The model does **not** simply switch every stressed location to a claim state.

### Conditional-independence limitation

In V2, the air-trigger draw and water-trigger draw are **conditionally independent given the simulated covariates**. The compound scenario creates non-additivity through an explicit frequency interaction and a compound-claim severity multiplier once both triggers are active, but it does **not** model residual air–water dependence with a copula, latent common shock, or spatial dependence process.

Students and instructors should state this limitation when interpreting compound-tail results. More advanced dependence structures remain optional extensions rather than capabilities of the core simulator.

## 9. Recommended actuarial use

### Frequency

Use:

```text
response = claim_count
offset   = log(exposure_years)
```

Suitable predictors include occupancy, region, continuous air/water variables, temperature, rainfall, drought, and mitigation indicators.

A Poisson GLM is the core teaching model. A Negative Binomial model may be introduced as a sensitivity or extension if dispersion diagnostics justify it.

### Conditional severity

Preferred V2 instructor/solution modelling response:

```text
claim_level_data.csv -> covered_loss
```

with a Gamma or Lognormal model.

Do not silently discard zero-paid claims to make `paid_loss` compatible with Gamma modelling.

### Expected loss

```text
Expected Claim Count = Expected Frequency × Exposure
Expected Covered Loss = Expected Claim Count × Expected Covered Severity
```

When converting to insurer-paid loss, policy financial terms must be simulated or otherwise applied consistently because deductibles and limits make `E[paid loss]` different from a simple linear transformation of expected covered severity.

### Indicated premium

Unless the instructor changes the assumptions:

- **Fixed expense per exposure-year:** 750
- **Variable expense ratio:** 25%
- **Profit / contingency provision:** 5%

```text
Indicated Premium =
(Expected Paid Loss + 750 × Exposure)
/
(1 - 0.25 - 0.05)
```

### Premium adequacy

```text
Adequacy Ratio = Current Premium / Indicated Premium
```

A ratio below 1.00 indicates underpricing under the scenario assumptions.

## 10. Mitigation decision language

The V2 classroom exercise evaluates a **finite set of candidate mitigation portfolios** under a fixed Climate Resilience Budget.

Students should therefore report:

> the feasible candidate portfolio with the lowest estimated `TVaR_99` among the candidate portfolios evaluated under the stated budget.

They should **not** claim that evaluating the supplied candidate set proves a global optimum over every possible mitigation allocation.

Supporting measures remain:

- expected-loss reduction;
- mitigation cost;
- benefit-cost ratio;
- VaR reduction;
- TVaR reduction; and
- post-mitigation premium adequacy.

The recommended initial classroom budget remains **8,000,000 currency units**, unless the instructor changes it.

## 11. Built-in generator validation checks

`data_script.py` fails immediately if any of the following V2 requirements are violated:

1. exactly 750 policy-location-year rows are not generated;
2. policy IDs are not unique;
3. exposures fall outside `(0, 1]`;
4. claim counts are negative, non-integer, or otherwise invalid;
5. any annual loss value is non-finite;
6. a claim is generated when no insured trigger exists;
7. claim-level `covered_loss` or `ground_up_loss` is non-positive or non-finite;
8. the BI waiting-period flag disagrees with interruption duration and waiting-period days;
9. BI loss is covered despite the waiting period not being met;
10. the deductible is not applied exactly once to the total covered claim;
11. a claim payment exceeds the per-claim policy limit;
12. annual ground-up, covered, or paid totals disagree with the supplementary claim-level table;
13. annual claim counts disagree with the number of claim-level records;
14. no legitimate zero-paid claims are present in the calibrated V2 baseline; or
15. a second generation with the same fixed seed differs from the first generation.

These checks make the corrected V2 DGP the authoritative definition inherited by the student notebook, solution notebook, case study, expected outputs, instructor guide, slides, and rubric.

## 12. Reproducibility

The fixed V2 seed is:

```text
ClimateTwin-CAS-2026-v2
```

The generator uses deterministic SHA-256 keyed pseudo-random streams rather than a stateful library RNG. For the same script and seed, repeated runs produce identical baseline policy-year and claim-level tables.

Run:

```bash
python data_script.py
```

The script writes:

```text
dataset.csv
claim_level_data.csv
```

in the same directory as the script and prints validation diagnostics after all checks pass.
